For American Fidelity's first-ever televised :30, a single script had been going in circles, so I used Claude as a writers' room. First I wrote down the real constraints: thirty seconds, a low shot count, no on-screen text the viewer has to read, nothing tied to any one industry, and the actual customer at the centre. Then we generated concept directions and pressure-tested each against that sheet, one pass at a time.

The ideas that survived came with their camera logic built in — locked-off frames, or a single continuous lateral move — so each could go straight to boards.

The same approach covers scripting: demo-driven teleprompter scripts for leadership videos, timed to read naturally in 90–120 seconds.
